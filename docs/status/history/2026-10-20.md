## The review of how a resolved xref is told

The maintainer settled 5.7's unattended call (D55) and admitted **M2**
(`../../design/out-of-band.md`), which landed ahead of 5.8.

- **Rule 1 is to read a refspec as unresolved where the link points at its own
  page or its fragment is the refspec.** A resolved link fails both: its
  component-version is not held, so it is never the linking page, and its
  fragment is the refspec's own, after its `#`, never the whole. Each test
  covers the other's hole. A `<base href>` moves the target, not the fragment.
  A refspec holding a character `url` percent-encodes in a fragment (space,
  `"`, `<`, `>`, `` ` ``) changes the fragment, not the target. There is no
  schema change.
- **Today's rule misjudges only a `<base href>` page**, in a live crawl too.
  An href `#…` and its page's row share the landing URL's canonical form, and
  `check-dir` seeds `foo/index.html` as `foo/` (`src/ci.rs`). Under a
  `<base href>` the xref is checked against the base page's anchors: usually
  `broken_anchor` with no reason, passing only where that page has none
  (`no_anchors`).
- Evidence: in `runs/p5.2-en-us-20261020/en-us.duckdb` and
  `runs/p4.10-en-us-20261019-2125/en-us.duckdb`, all 92 rows with
  `xref_refspec` point at their own page, their fragment equal to the refspec
  byte for byte.
- M2 changed verdicts only on a `<base href>` page, and en-US has none. D55
  carries the rule and `link_verdicts` (`src/store/views.rs`) the code.
- **Pickup:** 5.8.
